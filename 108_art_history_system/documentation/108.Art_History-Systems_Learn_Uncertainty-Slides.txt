KEY TAKEAWAYS

• Uncertainty is a constant in human history, but
our ways of relating to it change — from ritual
and faith, to calculation, to systems and
algorithms.

• Chance is not always the opposite of order.

Across history, it shifts from being a creative
force, to something suppressed, to something
designed into systems.

• Authorship emerges together with risk. When

divine and traditional guarantees collapse, the
artist becomes responsible for meaning — and
for being wrong.

• Modernity turns uncertainty into a
calculation, pricing risk through
probability, insurance, and
speculation — without eliminating
existential doubt.

• Scientific revolutions dismantle

determinism. Quantum physics
shows uncertainty as structural, not
a flaw of knowledge, and makes the
observer part of the system.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

• Mid-20th century science reframes

randomness.In cybernetics and
information theory, error and noise stop
being failures and become sources of
knowledge about how systems function.

• Systems can only regulate themselves
through deviation; instability is not the
opposite of order, but its condition.

• The artwork becomes the visible

operation of a system, not a finished
image.

• The work exists as a range of possible
states. Series replace unique objects;
each piece is one realization of a rule.

• The idea becomes a machine:

authorship moves from execution to
system design.

• LeWitt’s instructions function as

algorithms aimed at reproducibility;
Fluxus instructions trigger events
without a “correct” outcome.

CELLULAR AUTOMATA

No cell has knowledge of the whole.
There is no central control, no global plan, no “view from above.”
From these microscopic rules, macroscopic structures emerge.

Red — puffer-type breeder This is a moving
structure that: travels across the grid, leaves new
structures behind it, is not programmed to “create”
anything — it simply behaves this way. The puffer
does not know that it is producing anything.
Green — glider guns. These are stationary
structures that: regularly emit moving objects,
continue doing so indefinitely, as long as the system
exists. No one assigned them the task of “shooting.”
They do so inevitably because of the configuration of
the cells.

John Conway, The Game of Life

Blue — gliders. These are moving patterns: they
“glide” across the field, maintain their shape, can
collide with other structures and influence the
system. They are often referred to as the “particles”
of the Game of Life.

The artwork does not exist until minting.
The collector activates the algorithm, which generates a unique,

The artist gives up control.
Once published, the code executes and authenticates itself,

On-chain generative art is not an object or an AI model, but an

KEY TAKEAWAYS

• Generative art is defined by a way of
thinking, not by style or technology.

• Different generative systems ask different

questions. Procedural systems focus on
actions over time; algorithmic systems on
rules and reproducibility; agent-based
systems on emergence and behavior;
data-driven systems on translating reality;
on-chain systems on autonomy and
execution.

• With computers, systems become

executable. One algorithm can generate
infinite variations; form becomes a trace of
a procedure rather than an expressive
gesture.

• Across all these systems, the artwork is not
an object but a field of possible states.
Meaning does not precede form — it
emerges through execution.

Creation was never a solitary act of
expression; the environment was a
co-creative force.
What we see today is a continuation
of this logic, translated into a
technological context.
