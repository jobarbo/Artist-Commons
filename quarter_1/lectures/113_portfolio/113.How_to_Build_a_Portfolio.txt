A PORTFOLIO IS:
A portfolio is a designed viewing experience with a beginning, middle, and end.
A portfolio is an edited selection of your best work, designed for a specific viewer and purpose.

CURATE YOUR PORTFOLIO WITH RECENT WORK
If the portfolio is an exhibition, then editing it is a curatorial act.
That exhibition shouldn't be a hodge-podge.
Select work that speaks in chorus, with a unified voice.

A PORTFOLIO SHOULD REPRESENT YOUR CURRENT PRACTICE,
NOT YOUR ENTIRE HISTORY
The portfolio answers the question: Who is this artist today?
When older work appears, it should feel like part of the same trajectory. Not like an artifact from a
different identity.

Early-career artists should generally favor grouping by project and year.
More established artists can use weaving to communicate cohesion and depth.

WEAVING IS MORE PRONE TO FAILURE
Weaving is structurally more demanding. You're adding complexity, which assumes the work can sustain that complexity.
It requires consistency across your practice. If the relationship between projects isn't immediately clear, the portfolio will
feel chaotic. And you risk confusing reviewers, which makes your application an easy no.

In contrast, grouping by project is less likely to produce confusion.
Grouping's risks are different. It can make your art practice feel compartmentalized if you don't have clear connections
between your bodies of work.

A SIMPLE TEST
Does this work need to stand on its own to be understood?

If yes, group by project and year.

Does this work become clearer when seen in close connection with another?

If yes, weaving images and time-periods may be more appropriate.

Open with a central work, then present a constellation of supporting works.

WHEN DO TWO MEDIUMS BELONG IN THE SAME PORTFOLIO?
When they feel like different manifestations of the same inquiry.
The medium shifts. The investigation remains stable.
Example: An artist making paintings about fragmented architectural space, as well as sculptures with fragmented
architectural forms is clearly engaged in the same set of questions, even though the medium changes.
