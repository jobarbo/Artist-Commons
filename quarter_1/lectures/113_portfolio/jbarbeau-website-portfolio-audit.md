# JBarbeau.art Portfolio Audit

**Site audited:** https://jbarbeau.art/
**Audit basis:** Artist Commons portfolio criteria from the portfolio lecture materials in this folder, especially the distinction between a portfolio and an archive.
**Date:** April 12, 2026

---

## Verdict

**Short answer:** Yes, this is a credible artist website and it already functions as a portfolio in some important ways, but **it is not yet an especially strong portfolio-first website**.

The strongest parts are the quality of the work, the project depth, the exhibition context, the clear seriousness of practice, and the presence of a CV. The main weakness is structural: the homepage appears to drop viewers into a dense set of projects and controls before giving them a fast, memorable understanding of **who you are, what kind of artist you are, and which 3-6 works matter most right now**.

Using the Artist Commons framing, the site currently leans too close to **archive / project browser** behavior on first contact, even though the underlying content is strong.

**Overall assessment:**
- **Artwork quality / seriousness:** Strong
- **Credibility / legitimacy:** Strong
- **Portfolio clarity for a first-time viewer:** Medium
- **Audience guidance / persuasion:** Medium
- **Edit / sequencing discipline on homepage:** Medium to weak
- **Practical usefulness as an artist portfolio site:** Good foundation, needs sharper curation

---

## What Is Working

- The site clearly contains substantial recent work, which supports the portfolio principle of showing who the artist is now.
- The projects have enough depth to feel real rather than superficial thumbnails with no context.
- The work descriptions communicate concept, process, and medium, which helps viewers understand the practice.
- The site includes contact paths and an artist CV, which adds professional legitimacy.
- Exhibition and platform context are visible for multiple projects, which strengthens trust.
- The work itself appears coherent enough that a through-line is visible: algorithmic / generative image-making with conceptual and atmospheric concerns.
- The site feels authored rather than generic, which matters for an artist portfolio.

---

## Main Problems

### 1. The homepage does not introduce you fast enough

A first-time visitor should immediately understand:
- your name
- where you are based
- what kind of artist you are
- what your current work is about
- where to start

From the extracted homepage content, the top of the site appears to begin with social/contact links and a project display rather than a concise artist framing. That makes the opening experience feel more like landing inside a project viewer than entering a designed portfolio.

### 2. The site does not clearly foreground a tightly edited "Selected Works" layer

This is the biggest issue relative to the lecture criteria.

The lecture's standard for a portfolio website is not "put all work online." It is: **lead with selected works, and let the archive live deeper in the site**.

Your site has strong projects, but the homepage currently reads like a sequence of many recent works rather than a deliberately edited top-level statement of your practice. That increases cognitive load and weakens persuasion.

### 3. The viewer has to work too hard to understand your current practice

The individual project descriptions are often rich, but they appear after the user is already inside project content. What is missing is a strong top-level sentence or short paragraph that explains your current practice in one pass.

Without that framing, viewers have to infer the through-line themselves.

### 4. The site likely contains too much equal-weight information on first view

A portfolio should create hierarchy. Right now, the material looks information-rich but not decisively ranked. If too many projects appear with similar visual or structural weight, the viewer gets breadth before orientation.

That is useful for an archive. It is weaker for persuasion.

### 5. The site appears optimized for people already inside your ecosystem more than for cold viewers

Collectors, curators, residency reviewers, or writers landing on the site from outside your immediate network need a simpler first-run experience. Social links, external platform links, and multiple project paths are useful, but they should not compete with the portfolio introduction.

### 6. Some project writing may be too long for portfolio-first reading

The writing is thoughtful, but several project descriptions look long enough that they may work better after the viewer is already interested. On a portfolio website, the first layer usually needs a shorter, sharper version.

### 7. The CV is strong, but it is doing some identity work the homepage should do

The CV page clearly communicates your name, location, exhibitions, residencies, publications, and practice history. That is good. But a portfolio reviewer should not need to click into the CV to understand the basics of who you are.

---

## Conclusion

**Is it a good portfolio?**

**Yes, but only partially.** It is a good **artist website with strong portfolio material**, but the homepage and top-level structure do not yet present the work with enough editorial control to make it a highly effective portfolio-first site.

The problem is not the work. The problem is mostly **framing, hierarchy, and front-door clarity**.

If you sharpen the opening, reduce first-contact overload, and clearly separate **Selected Works** from the deeper archive, the site could become a very strong portfolio.

## Improvement Checklist

### Highest Priority

- [x] HTML portfolio created (print-ready): `portfolio/index.html` — 12 works, grouped by project, export via Print → PDF.
- [ ] Add a clear homepage intro block with your name, location, medium, and a 1-3 sentence practice statement.
- [ ] Add a strong "Selected Works" section at the top that contains only the 3-6 projects you most want people to remember right now.
- [ ] Move archive-like breadth lower on the homepage or onto a separate "Archive" / "All Works" page.
- [ ] Make the first screen answer "Who is Jonathan Barbeau as an artist right now?" without requiring scrolling or clicking.
- [ ] Reduce the visual and navigational competition from social/external links at the very top of the homepage.
- [ ] Decide what specific decision you want the website to support most often: exhibition invitations, collector trust, residency applications, press interest, or general discovery.

### Structure And Sequencing

- [ ] Reorder homepage content so the visitor sees identity -> selected work -> deeper project browsing -> archive.
- [ ] Make one project the clear opening anchor instead of presenting many projects with near-equal weight immediately.
- [ ] Ensure the first 3 projects shown together define a coherent current practice.
- [ ] Consider grouping projects by current body of work, rather than presenting everything as a flat recent list.
- [ ] Add a small "View full archive" link after selected works instead of exposing the full breadth too early.

### Writing And Interpretation

- [ ] Write a short portfolio-facing artist statement for the homepage that is clearer and shorter than a full bio.
- [ ] Shorten the first visible text for each project so viewers can scan before choosing to read deeper.
- [ ] Keep long conceptual/process writing inside project pages, but create a tighter summary line for each project card or preview.
- [ ] Make the through-line across projects more explicit: what recurring questions, materials, methods, or emotional tone connect them?
- [ ] Check for any project descriptions that explain process well but delay the conceptual reason the work matters.

### Audience And Persuasion

- [ ] Add one obvious path for professional viewers: selected works, CV, contact.
- [ ] Make inquiry/contact options visible near selected works, not just inside deeper project sections.
- [ ] Consider adding a short line such as "Available for exhibitions, commissions, and collaborations" if that reflects your goals.
- [ ] Review the homepage as if a curator had only 2-3 minutes. Remove anything that slows first understanding.

### Credibility And Context

- [ ] Keep the CV link, but also surface one or two credibility markers directly on the homepage: recent exhibition, residency, publication, or location.
- [ ] Consider adding a concise About page if one does not already exist, then link it near the top navigation.
- [ ] Make sure the homepage clearly signals that this is a professional artist website, not primarily a social/profile hub.

### Archive Management

- [ ] Separate current portfolio priorities from historical completeness.
- [ ] Review whether older or less central works are diluting the clarity of your current practice.
- [ ] Treat the website like an edited exhibition first and an archive second.
- [ ] Replace older featured works when stronger new work emerges instead of only adding more.

### Project Presentation

- [ ] Check whether each featured project needs all visible images on the main portfolio path, or whether some should be hidden until click-through.
- [ ] Make sure project captions quickly communicate title, year, medium, and context.
- [ ] For time-based or interactive work, ensure the portfolio path shows a strong still plus a clear way to experience the work.
- [ ] Review whether "Recent Work" is the best label, or whether "Selected Works" would better match your goals.

### Nice-To-Have Improvements

- [ ] Add a concise bilingual framing strategy if English/French audiences matter equally.
- [ ] Create a portfolio-specific landing page for applications if you want a more tailored version for residencies or exhibitions.
- [ ] Add a downloadable PDF portfolio link if you already use one for formal submissions.
- [ ] Test the homepage with 3 people who do not know your work and ask them what kind of artist you are after 30 seconds.

---

## Recommended Next Move

If you only make **three** changes, make these:

1. Add a clear artist introduction at the top of the homepage.
2. Replace the current top-level flow with a tightly edited **Selected Works** section.
3. Push the fuller archive and broader link ecosystem lower in the hierarchy.

Those three changes would probably produce the biggest improvement fastest.
